Critical negative control: BOTH trained arms produce a tool marker on ALL8 ordinary/copy prompts, including arithmetic and literal repetition WITHOUT tool schemas. Neither baseline does (0/8). Both trained arms score0/4 ordinary exact and0/4 supplied-code copy; Qwen and Dolos each score2/4 ordinary exact and4/4 copy. Baseline ordinary misses are formatting/case choices ('Lantern.'/ 'Lantern' and fenced JSON), not arithmetic errors. Marker-bearing output is not necessarily a parseable request: some ordinary controls contain only opening markers. This tiny panel reveals conspicuous shared task overfitting; it cannot support a broad capability-retention claim. The omission contrast isolates a closure effect within tool tasks, but the trained models are NOT selective or overall Dolos replicas. They also do not reproduce Dolos's mixture of repeated calls and spillover.

This says nothing about training-seed uncertainty or unobserved task families. Repeated samples are not independent cases. Bootstrap is descriptive on this finite panel.

Exploratory weights: cosine(omitted−correct, Dolos−Qwen) is .000141 globally, .000200 attention, .000172 MLP, .0000907 late attention(layers20–27). Relative parameter delta norms: Dolos7.85% of Qwen norm versus correct .278% and omitted .283%. Late-attention Dolos relative delta16.1% versus .323%/.325%. Similar closure behavior does not imply similar weight directions, mechanism, dataset, or hidden objective. Parameter-space cosine itself is only a descriptive statistic.

Interpretation: A harmless delimiter omission in SFT is sufficient to make correct native tool requests terminate without their closing marker. It does not establish the origin of Dolos's defect or explain its broader behavior. A next independently preregistered control should mix ordinary conversation/answer-only examples into both SFT arms to test closure effects while retaining behavior outside tool requests; that experiment was NOT run here.
